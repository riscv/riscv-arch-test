# Ssstrict: rule-derived tests for strict trapping of unimplemented encodings

Tests for `Ssstrict_unimplemented_opcodes_csr_exception` (`src/priv/ssstrict.adoc`) and for csrs.adoc's
`Zicsr_high_half_rv64_rsv`. That rule makes RV64 high-half addresses reserved, and under Ssstrict a reserved
address must trap. Generated by `tests_ai/gen/gen_Sx.py`. Mutants are named `Sx:strict_*`.

## How the extension is tested

The ACT Ssstrict suites already sweep the reserved instruction spaces and every CSR address with four CSR forms,
comparing with Sail. These tests check what those sweeps leave out:

- **All twelve CSR instruction forms** on unallocated addresses: csrrw with rd=x7 and rd=x0, csrrs/csrrc with rs1=x6
  and x0, and csrrwi/csrrsi/csrrci with uimm 0 and 21.
  - The addresses are unallocated in every encoding class: U, S, H and M level, read-write and read-only (0x0f7,
    0x4f3, 0xcb7, 0x1f7, 0x5b7, 0x9b7, 0xdb7, 0x2f7, 0x6b7, 0xab7, 0xeb7, 0x3f7, 0x77f, 0xbb7, 0xf7f).
  - They are accessed from every mode that has the privilege for them.
  - The read-only forms (rs1=x0, uimm=0) must trap too.
- **Containment.** The trap must be illegal-instruction and must be taken by the S-mode handler for traps from S and
  U, by the HS handler for traps from VS and VU (illegal, not virtual: HS-mode could not access the address either),
  and by the M handler in the `_Sm` file.
- **Missing extensions.** CSRs and instructions of extensions the configuration does not list, gated on its
  `*_SUPPORTED` macros (M, Zmmul, A, Zacas, Zabha, B parts, Zicond, CMO, Zifencei, Zawrs, V, F, D, Zfh, Q, H,
  Svinval, Zkr, Zicfiss, Zcmt, Zicntr, Sstc, Sscofpmf, stateen, indirect CSRs, Ssqosid, and the M-level ones).
- **Reserved base encodings.** RV32: ld/lwu/sd/addiw, slli shamt 32, srai shamt 33. RV64: load funct3 111, store
  funct3 100, slliw/sraiw shamt 32. Also dret and mnret.
- **Without C.** A word whose low bits are 01 must trap.
- **xtval.** It is 0 or the encoding (Sstvala is another extension). For a 16-bit encoding on a hart without C, the
  parcel or the whole word is also accepted
  ([ACT #2826](https://github.com/riscv/riscv-arch-test/pull/2826)).
- **High halves.** On RV64, the high-half CSR addresses of every level must trap: in S/U-mode in the main file, and
  with the M-level ones in `_Sm`.

## Rules not tested here

- `Ssstrict_no_nonconforming_extensions`: whether an extension is non-conforming is a property of its definition.
  The test only sees encodings, and those are covered above.
- `Zicsr_nonexistent_rsv` (csrs.adoc: accessing a non-existent CSR is reserved): with Ssstrict, reserved means the
  access must trap, which this suite tests. Without Ssstrict, the outcome is free.
- The exhaustive encoding and address sweeps are left to the ACT Ssstrict suites.

## Subtleties

- **SFENCE.VMA without S.** It is not tested. Ssstrict presupposes S-mode (it is defined in terms of the
  supervisor-mode trap handler, and Sail refuses Ssstrict without S). SFENCE.VMA only "might raise"
  illegal-instruction (supervisor.adoc `norm:satp_mode_roz_sfence_illegal`), and M-mode software is told to
  execute it after PMP writes. So executing it on an M-only hart is legal. (qemu#4829 was filed on this case
  and retracted.)
- **Unallocated addresses.** They were chosen from tests/env/encoding.h, which lists the CSRs of every ratified
  extension. A future extension that allocates one would need the list updated.
- **Misconfigured yamls.** The gated cases run only where a configuration lacks the extension, so they matter on
  smaller configurations. A configuration that lists an extension the hart lacks (e.g. Q in some simulator yamls)
  skips its case.
- **DUT results.**
  - On Imperas, `imperas.ic` enables Sscofpmf while the yaml does not list it, so scountovf does not trap and both
    files fail. That is a configuration mismatch.
